Applies to Original Medicare only. **Does not apply to Medicare Advantage (Part C) or Part D** — see §5.

## 3. Medicare FFS — evidence preclusion (the rule that decides most cases)

**This is the single highest-leverage rule for a provider-side denial workflow: the record is effectively closed at the QIC.**

| Rule | Detail | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| Full-and-early presentation | Absent good cause, failure to submit all evidence — including documentation requested in the notice of redetermination — before the reconsideration is issued **precludes subsequent consideration of that evidence**. | [42 CFR 405.966(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR533a2e506b78025/section-405.966) |
| Who this binds | Providers, suppliers, and beneficiaries **represented by** a provider or supplier. | [42 CFR 405.966](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR533a2e506b78025/section-405.966) |
| Who is exempt | Beneficiaries filing on their own behalf and State Medicaid Agencies are not required to comply with 405.966(a). Beneficiaries represented by a provider or supplier **are** bound. | [42 CFR 405.966(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR533a2e506b78025/section-405.966) |
| Cost of late evidence at Level 2 | Each submission of additional evidence after the reconsideration request automatically extends the QIC's 60-day clock by up to 14 calendar days. Does not apply to timely submission of documentation the QIC specifically requested, unless it was originally requested in the redetermination notice. | [42 CFR 405.966(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR533a2e506b78025/section-405.966) |
| New evidence at Level 3 | Evidence from a provider, supplier, or provider/supplier-represented beneficiary not submitted before the QIC reconsideration **must be accompanied by a statement explaining why**. Without that statement, **the evidence will not be considered.** | [42 CFR Part 405 Subpart I, ALJ Hearings](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR18c5ab80164e759) |
| Good-cause screening at Level 3 | The ALJ examines new evidence submitted with the hearing request, or within 10 calendar days of receiving the notice of hearing, to determine good cause. Good cause exists, for example, where the evidence is material to an issue addressed in the QIC's reconsideration and that issue was not identified as material prior to the ALJ hearing procedures. | [42 CFR Part 405 Subpart I, ALJ Hearings](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR18c5ab80164e759) |

**Operational consequence:** any medical record, physician attestation, order, signature log, or LCD-matching documentation must land at Level 1 or Level 2. Holding it back for the ALJ is not a strategy; it is a forfeiture.

## 4. Medicare FFS — late filing, and appeal vs. reopening

### 4a. Good cause to extend a missed filing deadline

| Rule | Detail | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| Extension available | If the 120-day redetermination window has expired and the party shows good cause, the contractor **may** extend it. File the extension request with the contractor and include supporting evidence. | [42 CFR 405.942(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR3ec6343ada14c59/section-405.942) |
| Factors weighed | Circumstances that prevented timely filing; whether contractor action misled the party; physical, mental, educational or linguistic limitations (including limited English); whether the contractor gave incorrect or incomplete filing information; whether the party never received the determination notice; whether the request was sent in good faith to a government agency within the limit but reached the right contractor late. | [42 CFR 405.942(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR3ec6343ada14c59/section-405.942) |
| Effect on the decision clock | Where an extension is granted, the 60-day decision clock starts on the date the contractor receives the late-filed request **or** the date the extension is granted, whichever is later. | [42 CFR 405.942](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR3ec6343ada14c59/section-405.942) |
| Level 2 analogue | A parallel timeframe/extension provision exists for reconsideration requests. | [42 CFR 405.962](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR533a2e506b78025/section-405.962) |

### 4b. Reopening — the alternate route when the appeal window is gone

Reopening is **not** an appeal and carries no appeal rights of its own, but it is often the only remaining path.

| Window | Standard | Source |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Within 1 year of the initial determination or redetermination | **Any reason** | [42 CFR 405.980](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR70fab268832aef1/section-405.980) |
| Within 4 years | **Good cause** per 42 CFR 405.986 — new and material evidence not available or known at the time that may change the result, **or** evidence already considered that shows an obvious error on its face | [42 CFR 405.980](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR70fab268832aef1/section-405.980) |
| At any time | Reliable evidence that the determination was procured by **fraud or similar fault** (as defined at 42 CFR 405.902) | [42 CFR 405.980](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-405/subpart-I/subject-group-ECFR70fab268832aef1/section-405.980) |

Reopening is discretionary on the contractor's part; see also [Medicare Claims Processing Manual Ch. 34](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/clm104c34.pdf).

## 6. Commercial payers — internal appeals

### 6a. The critical framing

There are **two distinct things** commonly called a "commercial appeal," and they have different deadlines and different legal footing:

| Track | Whose right | Deadline source | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Member / claimant appeal** under ERISA and the ACA | The plan participant or beneficiary (a provider may act only as an **authorized representative**) | Federal regulation — floors are mandatory | 29 CFR 2560.503-1; 45 CFR 147.136 |
| **Provider claim dispute / payment reconsideration** | The provider, under its participation agreement | **Contract- and payer-specific.** No federal deadline. | See §6d for observed ranges |

**Assignment of benefits is not the same as appeal standing.** DOL guidance states an assignment of benefits by a claimant is generally limited to assignment of the claimant's right to *receive a benefit payment* under the terms of the plan. To pursue the member's ERISA appeal, obtain an authorized-representative designation, which a plan may not preclude. ([DOL, Benefit Claims Procedure Regulation FAQs](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/faqs/benefit-claims-procedure-regulation); [29 CFR 2560.503-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XXV/subchapter-G/part-2560/section-2560.503-1))

### 6b. ERISA group health plan floors — 29 CFR 2560.503-1

These are **minimums the plan must at least meet**; a plan document may be more generous, never less.

| Item | Requirement | Source |

### 6d. Observed commercial provider-appeal deadlines — contract-specific, verify per contract

**These are not rules. They are published defaults from payer provider manuals, and an individual participation agreement, state prompt-pay statute, or delegated-entity arrangement overrides them.** Always read the specific agreement and the remittance's own appeal-rights language.

## 7. Medicaid

### 7a. Who a fair hearing is for

42 CFR Part 431 Subpart E is titled **"Fair Hearings for Applicants and Beneficiaries."** It is the beneficiary's remedy for an adverse action on eligibility or services. It is **not** the general forum for a provider's claim-payment dispute. ([42 CFR Part 431 Subpart E](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-431/subpart-E))

### 7b. Medicaid fee-for-service — state fair hearing

## 8. Quick routing table

| If the payer is… | First action | Deadline to beat | Hard gate to watch |
|------------------|--------------|------------------|--------------------|
| Original Medicare (A/B) | Redetermination to the MAC, form CMS-20027 | 120 days from receipt of the RA/MSN (notice date + 5) | Submit **all** evidence by the QIC stage or it is precluded (42 CFR 405.966) |
| Original Medicare, appeal window already missed | Request a reopening | 1 year for any reason; 4 years with good cause | Reopening is discretionary and confers no appeal right |
| Medicare Advantage | Plan reconsideration | 60 days from the organization determination notice | Non-contract provider **must** file a Waiver of Liability |
| Commercial, provider-side dispute | Payer's claim reconsideration per the participation agreement | Assume **60 days** until the contract is verified; 180 days is the most common contractual figure | The contract, not federal law, sets this |
| Commercial, member-side / medical necessity | Member ERISA internal appeal via authorized-representative designation | At least 180 days from the adverse benefit determination | AOB alone does not confer appeal standing |
| Commercial, internal appeals exhausted | External review (state process, or HHS federal process) | 4 months from the final internal adverse determination | Grandfathered plans are largely exempt, except NSA-scope items |
| Medicaid managed care | Plan appeal (the only plan level) | 60 calendar days from the adverse benefit determination notice | Then 90–120 days (state-set) to request the fair hearing |
| Medicaid FFS | State fair hearing | Up to 90 days from the mailing of the notice of action | Fair hearings are the beneficiary's remedy, not a provider payment forum |

## Open questions

- **Whether the ERISA "special circumstances" extension at 29 CFR 2560.503-1(i)(1) applies to group health post-service appeal decisions.** Retrieved text describing a 60-day extension appeared alongside the general (i)(1) rule rather than the group-health-specific (i)(3). The group health floors in §6b are sourced; the availability of an extension on top of them is not confirmed. Read 29 CFR 2560.503-1(i) in full before relying on any extension.
- **Whether the ERISA appeal-filing floor for group health is 180 days in every case.** DOL's group health publication states 180 days; one retrieved excerpt cited a general "at least 60 days" figure that appears to be the non-group-health rule at (h)(2). Treat 180 days as the group health floor and verify against the specific plan document.
- **The exact enumerated required elements of a Medicare redetermination request** under Medicare Claims Processing Manual Ch. 29 §310. Retrieval confirmed: must be in writing; CMS-20027 or a dated, signed written statement; beneficiary name, Medicare number, reasons for appealing, supporting evidence. The full itemized list (including whether specific HCPCS/CPT codes and dates of service are mandatory elements versus recommended) was not retrieved. Read [Ch. 29](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/clm104c29.pdf) directly.
- **Whether the 180-day QIC filing window runs from presumed receipt (notice date + 5) or from the date of the redetermination notice.** The 5-day presumption was confirmed explicitly for the initial determination, the reconsideration decision, and the Council decision, but not explicitly for the redetermination decision. Assume notice date + 5 conservatively; do not rely on the extra 5 days.
- **Federal deadline for Medicaid *provider* claim-payment appeals.** None located. 42 CFR Part 431 Subpart E covers applicants and beneficiaries; 42 CFR Part 438 Subpart F covers enrollees. Provider payment disputes appear to be entirely state plan / state law / contract driven. Verify per state and per MCO contract.
- **State-by-state external review deadlines that diverge from 4 months.** The NAIC model and the federal standard are both 4 months, but the model is adopted state-by-state and enacting statutes may differ. Verify against the specific state's insurance code.
- **Medicare Advantage Level 1 decision timeframes** (standard vs. expedited organization determinations and reconsiderations under 42 CFR 422.590) were not retrieved in detail. Read [42 CFR 422.590](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-M/section-422.590) before asserting them.
- **Whether the ALJ decision timeframe for a Part C or Part D appeal matches the FFS 90 days.** Not confirmed for the managed care track.
- **CY2027 amount-in-controversy thresholds.** Not yet published as of the retrieval date. The CY2027 notice is normally issued in the autumn preceding the year; re-check the Federal Register before January 1, 2027, since a request filed on or after that date is measured against the new figure.
- **Current Part C IRE contact details.** CMS guidance indicates the IRE changed from MAXIMUS to C2C effective 05/01/2026. The retrieval date is after that transition, but the specific C2C mailing address and fax were not retrieved. Confirm on [CMS, Reconsideration by Part C IRE](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/appeals-grievances/managed-care/review-part-c-independent-entitiy) before submitting.
